# Fictional live sample, not a benchmark

## Path A: Run a paid pilot with two existing customers
**Potential benefit:** Testing the new reporting service with two customers may provide valuable feedback on the service's functionality and pricing. This could help refine the offering before a broader launch, potentially increasing customer satisfaction and retention.

**Main trade-off:** Limiting the pilot to two customers might restrict the amount of data collected on demand and customer preferences. This could result in a less comprehensive understanding of the overall market need when the service is eventually rolled out.

**Assumptions to test:** The two customers will provide constructive feedback that is representative of the broader customer base. Additionally, the pilot will not overextend the delivery team's capacity.

**Evidence needed:** Information on the delivery time per report is crucial, as well as insights into what customers are willing to pay for the service. Feedback from the pilot customers will also be necessary to gauge interest and satisfaction.

**Reversibility:** Running a pilot is a reversible step; if the feedback is unfavourable or the service is not well-received, adjustments can be made before a wider rollout.

## Path B: Roll it out to all 30 customers immediately
**Potential benefit:** A full rollout could generate immediate revenue from all customers and might create momentum for the new reporting service. It allows the delivery team to engage with the entire customer base at once, potentially increasing retention across the board.

**Main trade-off:** Without prior testing, there is a risk of overwhelming the delivery team if demand exceeds expectations. Additionally, launching without feedback may lead to customer dissatisfaction if the service does not meet their needs.

**Assumptions to test:** Customers will respond positively to the new reporting service, and the delivery team can manage the workload effectively without compromising quality.

**Evidence needed:** Understanding customer pricing expectations and delivery time per report is essential. Insights into the overall demand for the service would also be beneficial.

**Reversibility:** A full rollout is less reversible; if the service fails to meet expectations, it may damage customer trust and retention.

## Blind spots
1. **Market demand:** There is uncertainty regarding how many customers are genuinely interested in the new reporting service beyond the two who have expressed interest. This matters because it impacts whether a pilot or full rollout is more effective.
2. **Capacity of the delivery team:** It is unclear how the team will manage the workload if all 30 customers engage with the service simultaneously. Understanding this capacity is crucial to avoid potential service failures.

## Second-order effects
1. **Customer feedback loop:** Running a pilot could establish a feedback loop, allowing for ongoing improvements and adjustments based on customer input, thereby enhancing future engagement.
2. **Team dynamics:** The choice between a pilot and a full rollout might influence team morale and workload management, affecting productivity and employee satisfaction.

## A test before committing
Consider conducting a brief survey with all customers to gauge their interest in the new reporting service and their willingness to pay. A success criterion could be that at least 50% express interest and provide pricing feedback. If less than this threshold is met, it may warrant reconsidering the pilot approach.

## A question worth answering
What specific features or outcomes do customers expect from the new reporting service that would enhance their retention?
